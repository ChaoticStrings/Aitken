Contractor-to-road data can be had openly and legally for national highways and some lender-funded roads, partly for Karnataka, and not yet for Pune's city roads. NHIDCL alone names contractors on 358 civil road works to 31 May 2026, and the World Bank and ADB publish award data under CC BY licences. The volume that would make this "at scale" sits in state e-procurement award pages behind CAPTCHAs, which this run did not touch.

This run extended the 18 seed rows to 30 and re-ranked all of them by expected road-level links per unit of effort and legal risk. Status now stands at 5 Verified, 17 Partly verified and 8 Unverified. Seed rows for MoRTH Data Lake and OpenStreetMap moved from Verified to Partly verified, because only a snapshot was inspected, not the live source. Nothing is archived on the Wayback Machine yet: the sandbox cannot reach it, so that is the first job on your network.

Prepared 2026-10-17. Source-gathering run for the Crater/Artemis feasibility work. This is a register of where data can be obtained openly and legally. It is not a scraping run and contains no scraped data.

## How to read this

RoV means return on value: how much of the gap between road geometry and the contractor responsible for it a source closes, per unit of effort and legal risk. Rank 1 is the highest.

Status is one of three values. Verified means the data was inspected directly in the feasibility sessions. Partly verified means a snapshot or sample was seen, but not the live source. Unverified means the source is listed from prior knowledge or search results and has not been reached.

## Sources ruled out

- Third-party tender aggregators and paid databases (infralens, BidsFactory, DevelopmentAid, CRISIL Quantix). Terms and provenance are unclear, or access is paid or login-gated. infralens claims 53,95,265 mirrored awards; use that only as evidence that the volume exists.
- Any scraper that cracks CAPTCHAs, including the method CivicDataLab used for Himachal Pradesh. Do not run or copy it.
- eMARG contractor-per-road data. It needs a registered stakeholder login, and registering under a role you do not hold is ruled out.
- Contractor enlistment registers with individuals' names, addresses and phone numbers (Punjab PWD). They hold personal data and no road link.
- Contact fields in the NHIDCL and toll-plaza files (emails, names, phone numbers). Drop them at ingest.

## Handling rules for this run

- Open access only: public pages, anonymous endpoints and open licences. No CAPTCHA solving, no logins, no registering under a role the user does not hold.
- Check robots.txt and terms of use before any request. Rate-limit requests and identify the client with a real contact address in the User-Agent.
- Personal names: the Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023 applies. Keep firm names in the register. Individual names stay out of any public-facing output and need legal review first.
- Archive every source at citation time, through the Wayback Machine, before it is cited.
- Record each source's licence per file. ODbL share-alike applies to derived databases.
- Do not register on any portal unless no other route exists. Log every registration.

For counsel: DPDP Act section 3(c)(ii)(B) excludes personal data that a person is legally obliged to publish, and CVC Office Order 13/3/05 tells central bodies to post monthly contract summaries with contractor name and value. Whether either covers re-use here is a legal question. Both were read through secondary copies only.

## Next steps for your own network

1. Before any download: put a monitored contact in the User-Agent, read robots.txt and terms for nhai.gov.in, nhidcl.com, omms.nic.in and kppp.karnataka.gov.in, then archive every URL cited here with Save Page Now and record the snapshot links.
2. Pull the highway files: NHAI status PDFs and NHIDCL monthly PDFs. Extract the tables, drop email and phone columns, keep firm names, and set individual names aside for counsel. Try the toll-plaza anchors (rank 21) to place chainage on geometry.
3. Open one public OMMAS report and note whether it shows the contractor, asks for a CAPTCHA or blocks automated access. If it shows contractors with no gate, start with Maharashtra and join to Geosadak. If it needs a login or CAPTCHA, stop there.
4. Pull the World Bank India awards by API and the ADB XLSX, filter to roads, and record the attribution lines. Rerun the Vonter pipeline with a wider KPPP department scope.
5. For Pune: look for the PMRDA defect-liability list on pmrda.gov.in. If it names no contractors, file RTI requests with PMC and PMRDA for defect-liability registers, work orders and award letters, asking for road name, contractor and defect-liability end date.
6. Put one question to counsel: whether the DPDP Act and the CVC publication order let you re-use individuals' names that public bodies publish. Until then, publish firm names only.
